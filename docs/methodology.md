# Methodology: Biomedical Signal Processing & Machine Learning

## 1. Biomedical Signal Acquisition & Noise Sources
Electrocardiogram (ECG) recordings record the biopotential differences generated by cardiac myocardial depolarization and repolarization. Real-world recordings contain several artifact sources:
- **Powerline Interference**: 50 Hz (Europe/Asia) or 60 Hz (North America) electromagnetic pickup.
- **Baseline Wander**: 0.15 Hz to 0.5 Hz low-frequency drifts caused by patient respiration and movement.
- **Electromyographic (EMG) Noise**: High-frequency muscle tremors (>45 Hz).
- **Electrode Contact Noise & Motion Artifacts**: Step changes and baseline jumps.

---

## 2. Signal Preprocessing Pipeline

### 2.1 Powerline Interference Suppression
An Infinite Impulse Response (IIR) notch filter is applied:
$$H_{notch}(z) = \frac{1 - 2\cos(\omega_0)z^{-1} + z^{-2}}{1 - 2r\cos(\omega_0)z^{-1} + r^2 z^{-2}}$$
where $\omega_0 = 2\pi f_0 / f_s$ with $f_0 = 60\text{ Hz}$, $f_s = 360\text{ Hz}$, and Quality Factor $Q = 30$.

### 2.2 Butterworth Bandpass Filtering
A 3rd-order zero-phase Butterworth bandpass filter ($0.5\text{ Hz} - 45.0\text{ Hz}$) removes high-frequency muscle noise and residual low-frequency drift without shifting the phase of the QRS complex.

### 2.3 Cascaded Two-Stage Median Baseline Correction
Following the Meyer and de Chazal standard:
1. **Stage 1 (200 ms window)**: Removes P-wave and QRS complex spikes.
2. **Stage 2 (600 ms window)**: Removes T-wave components.
3. The resulting smooth baseline $\hat{b}[n]$ is subtracted from the signal:
   $$x_{clean}[n] = x[n] - \hat{b}[n]$$
This preserves ST-segment elevation/depression without filter ringing.

### 2.4 R-Peak Detection & Beat Segmentation
- **Pan-Tompkins Algorithm (1985)**: Bandpass (5-15 Hz) $\to$ 5-point derivative $\to$ non-linear squaring $\to$ moving-window integration (150 ms) $\to$ adaptive thresholding with 200 ms physiological refractory lockout.
- **Segmentation Window**: 250 samples (~694 ms @ 360 Hz) centered on the R-peak:
  - 90 samples (~250 ms) pre-R covering the P-wave and PR-segment.
  - 160 samples (~444 ms) post-R covering the ST-segment and T-wave.

## 3. Feature Extraction Engineering

### 3.1 Time-Domain Statistical & Morphological Features
For each heartbeat $x$:
- **Moments**: Mean $\mu$, Variance $\sigma^2$, Skewness $\gamma_1 = E\left[\left(\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\right)^3\right]$, Kurtosis $\beta_2 = E\left[\left(\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\right)^4\right] - 3$.
- **Energy Metrics**: Total Energy $E = \sum x_i^2$, Root Mean Square (RMS).
- **Zero-Crossing Rate (ZCR)**: Frequency of baseline transitions.
- **Energy Asymmetry Ratio**: Ratio of pre-R energy (atrial) to post-R energy (ventricular).
- **QRS Slopes**: Differential rising and falling slopes ($\Delta V / \Delta t$) around the R-peak.

### 3.2 Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) Multi-Resolution Analysis
The Daubechies-4 (`db4`) wavelet family is employed because its compact support and asymmetric scaling function closely match human QRS complexes.
Decomposition down to Level 4 produces:
- Approximation band $cA_4$ ($0 - 11.25\text{ Hz}$)
- Detail bands $cD_4$ ($11.25 - 22.5\text{ Hz}$), $cD_3$ ($22.5 - 45\text{ Hz}$), $cD_2$ ($45 - 90\text{ Hz}$), $cD_1$ ($90 - 180\text{ Hz}$).
- For each subband: Energy $E_j$, relative energy $p_j = E_j / E_{total}$, and Shannon Wavelet Entropy:
  $$H_{wav} = -\sum_{j} p_j \log_2(p_j)$$

## 4. Classification Architectures

### 4.1 Classical Machine Learning: Random Forest & SVM
- **Random Forest**: Ensemble of 100 decorrelated decision trees with balanced class weighting to overcome the 80:1 clinical class imbalance.
- **Support Vector Machine**: Radial Basis Function (RBF) kernel with Platt probability scaling.

### 4.2 Deep Learning: 1D Convolutional Neural Network (1D-CNN)
End-to-end waveform feature extraction directly on raw normalized beats:
- Conv1D (32 filters, kernel 7) $\to$ BatchNorm $\to$ ReLU $\to$ MaxPool1D(2) $\to$ Dropout(0.1)
- Conv1D (64 filters, kernel 5) $\to$ BatchNorm $\to$ ReLU $\to$ MaxPool1D(2) $\to$ Dropout(0.2)
- Conv1D (128 filters, kernel 3) $\to$ BatchNorm $\to$ ReLU $\to$ MaxPool1D(2) $\to$ Dropout(0.2)
- Adaptive Average Pooling (1) $\to$ Dense(64) $\to$ Dropout(0.25) $\to$ Dense(5, Softmax).
- Optimization: Adam ($\text{lr}=10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$), ReduceLROnPlateau scheduler, CrossEntropyLoss with inverse frequency class weights.
